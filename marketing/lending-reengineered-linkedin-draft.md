A lot of lending today still runs on **people manually reading documents**. APIs like GST, bureau, and bank statements cover part of the picture, but a huge chunk of underwriting is **rent agreements, sales data, financials, and 360 data that no stack can cleanly parse** — so a human sits there doing QC, page by page.

That model is changing fast. About time.

Credit decisions are finally getting smart — **reading hundreds of signals, not one bureau score**. And fast: **digital onboarding turned days into minutes**. People who were invisible to the system are finally getting seen.

Then the RBI walked into the room.

Their draft **Guidance on Model Risk Management (2026)** says: if a model makes your lending decisions, you govern it like one. The headline everyone's talking about is the **"kill switch"** — a way to override, pause, or shut a model down, with a human able to step in. Plus **explainability, bias testing, and constant monitoring**.

I think this is exactly right. **AI in lending can't just be clever. It has to be answerable.**

When decisions are fast, smart and accountable, lending stops being a gatekeeper and becomes an enabler.

AI gave lending a new engine. The RBI just reminded everyone it also needs brakes and a steering wheel.
